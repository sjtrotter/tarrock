# Interlude — The Amendment

*From the Registry Hall's standing files, the Bastion. Reproduced in order, with stamps
and marginalia as found.*

*  *  *

**PETITION FOR CORRECTION OF PUBLIC RECORD**
*Filed by: Goodwife Nan Ostler, of the second terrace*
*Concerning: the memorial plaque, east colonnade, honouring the late Hale Ostler*
*Nature of correction sought: one (1) word. The plaque records the deceased's guild as
Wrights. The deceased was, for the whole of his working life, a member in good standing
of the Coopers. The error is understood to be a clerk's slip, made the week of the
Stall, and has stood uncorrected since.*
*Nature of restitution sought: none. Correction only.*

DEFERRED. — *stamp, Registry Hall, first filing*

DEFERRED. — *stamp, refiled*

DEFERRED. — *stamp, refiled*

*[a great many further stamps follow, each identical, each dated by gong-count rather
than day in the old fashion, running the full width of the sheet and down the margin and,
where the margin ran out, onto a second sheet pinned behind the first. The last stamp
before the file breaks off reads, like all the others:]*

DEFERRED.

*  *  *

**AMENDMENT NO. 1**
*Being the first instance, in the whole history of the Bastion's Registry, of a public
record altered from its original filing.*

Petition of Goodwife Nan Ostler (see attached, three hundred and some years of filings,
which this office declines to count precisely, out of what it can only describe as
mercy toward itself) is **GRANTED**.

Let the record show: the deceased Hale Ostler was a Cooper. Let a plate be cut and set
beside the existing plaque, bearing the correction, that both the error and the truth
may stand together in the colonnade, since it is this office's considered view that a
truth arriving late loses nothing by arriving beside the mistake it corrects, rather than
erasing it. The old plaque is not removed. The old plaque was never the enemy. It was
only wrong, for a long time, in a city that had no drawer marked *wrong* to put it in.

We have that drawer now.

— A., Clerk of the Registry

*[The seal beneath the signature is struck crooked — canted a full quarter-turn off the
true, the ink smeared at one corner where the stamp was lifted a half-instant early. A
note, in the same careful hand, is added below it, small, as though the writer expected
to be asked and preferred to have the answer ready:]*

*I am aware the seal sits crooked. I am aware I could strike it again, true, and no one
would think the less of me for it. I find I do not wish to. Every seal I pressed for
three hundred years landed exactly where the law required and corrected nothing at all.
This one is the first that ever did anything worth doing, and I would rather it wore the
mark of the hand that finally let it. Leave it crooked. File this note with the rest.*

*  *  *

**REGISTRY NOTICE — TO ALL CITIZENS OF THE BASTION**
*Re: the return of certain irregularities, so-called "petty crime," previously unknown
to this city within living or recorded memory*

The Registry wishes to advise the public, in the interest of transparency and because
several citizens have written in some alarm demanding to know what is to be *done* about
it, that the Bastion's first theft in three centuries has occurred, and that the matter
has been handled, after a fashion, and that the public may rest as easy as a public can
reasonably be expected to rest under the circumstances.

The item taken was one (1) apple, from a stall on the second terrace, in full view of
its owner, at a time of day with no fewer than nine witnesses. The offender did not flee.
The offender did not, in point of fact, appear to have formed any plan whatsoever beyond
the taking, and upon completing the theft was observed standing quite still, apple in
hand, with the visible expression of a man who has climbed a stair expecting one further
step and found none. He offered no resistance to the two guards who eventually converged
upon him, largely because neither guard could recall the correct procedure either, there
being no living guard who had ever arrested anyone, and a certain amount of the ensuing
delay is attributable to both parties consulting each other, at length, on what was
meant to happen next.

The Registry records this as **the first offence, by the first offender, in every
possible sense the city has**, and enters his name as Loft, called *Sixpence* on his own
street for reasons no clerk has yet succeeded in establishing. There is some feeling among
the clerks that a man's very first attempt at wrongdoing, made in good faith and appalling
technique, is not quite the same class of record as a seasoned villainy, and the file has
been marked accordingly, in pencil. He is warned.
He is, by several accounts, already regretting the whole undertaking rather more than
the apple was worth. The Registry wishes him, in the driest terms available to it, better
luck, or failing that, less of it.

*  *  *

**AMENDMENT — BLANK FORM**
*Printed this season, in quantity, for the first time in the Registry's history*

To be completed by the petitioner in full. Amendments will be processed in the order
received, subject to the ordinary business of a city that is, the Registry is pleased to
report, no longer required to pretend it has none.

*Printed at the foot of every copy, in the same small careful hand as the seal note
above, a line that was not asked for by any authority and was set into the plate anyway:*

*It could be corrected. So we corrected it.*

*  *  *

Appended to the file, beneath the final blank form, in handwriting that does not match
the clerk's — rounder, older, pressed hard enough that the nib has caught the paper twice
— a line was found some while after the Amendment closed, added by no authority anyone
could name and removed by none:

*Thirty years I have filed that word. It was never really the word I was keeping. It was
somewhere to put him, of a morning, that wasn't just missing him. I don't rightly know
where I'm to put him now it's done. I expect I'll find somewhere. I expect that's the
whole of what everyone in this city is about to have to learn to do.*

*— N. Ostler*
