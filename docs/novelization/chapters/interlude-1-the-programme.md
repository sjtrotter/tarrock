# Interlude — The Programme

**THE PRESTIGE**

*proudly, for once and only once, presents —*

**THE VERY LAST NIGHT**

*Being the Genuine, Actual, No-Fooling Final Performance of the whole Company, prior to
Departure upon the Open Road. Management begs your indulgence: for three hundred years
every night was billed as unforgettable and meant it as a figure of speech. This one we
mean literally, and we are ourselves a little unsteady about it.*

*  *  *

Ladies! Gentlemen! Small enthusiastic dogs, if any should present themselves at the gate
with the correct number of legs! You are cordially invited to the one show this
establishment has ever run that is actually, finally, allowed to end.

You have seen us before. Of course you have. You have seen us nine hundred times, in the
same seats, and we are ashamed to say we never once noticed how tired you must have got
of noticing. That debt is paid the only way it can be — not by refunding you the years
(we asked; it cannot be done; see NOTICE, below) but by giving you, at long last, a last
act. A door that shuts. A curtain that comes down and *stays* down, so that when you go
home tonight it will be because the show is over, and not because you simply ran out of
evening.

The bill in full: Tansy Quill's glass, one final time, and this once she promises to let
somebody catch her tired on purpose. Perrin Loom's Menagerie, paper beasts and all,
donated afterward to any child who can carry a paper elephant without crying (results
expected to be mixed). Old Tomkin's five clubs, on the wire, and — he has given
Management leave to print this — he means to drop one. On purpose. Once. For the joy of
it. Marigold Fen will not be reading fortunes tonight. She says she already knows how
the evening goes, and for the first time in three hundred years she is glad she does.

And in the ring: Wicke. Just Wicke. No Bataleur, no top hat held at the angle of a man
who has never once been surprised. He asked that this be printed plainly, and Management,
this once, obliged him.

*  *  *

**NOTICE TO PATRONS, RE: RAIN-CHECK TOKENS**

The management wishes to apologise, sincerely and at some length, for the matter of the
rain-check tokens. It has come to our attention that a number of patrons are in
possession of tokens issued against performances cancelled on account of weather,
weather here being a thing that, until quite recently, this establishment did not
technically have. We are aware that some of these tokens predate the founding of several
neighbouring towns. We are aware that one was reportedly found sewn into the lining of a
coat belonging to a gentleman's great-great-grandmother. We will honour every one of
them, at the door, tonight only, though we confess we no longer recall precisely what a
rain-check token entitles the bearer *to*, and would be obliged if the bearer would tell
us. A seat, presumably. We have those. We have rather a lot of them, going spare, as of
tomorrow.

**NOTICE, RE: THE MATTER OF MR CUTTER VOSS**

Patrons of the rival sideshow situated regrettably close to our own gate are advised
that Mr Voss's strongman act is, as ever, entirely genuine, and that the management
extends him every professional respect, and that none of the foregoing prevents Flick
from being asked, this very night, whether Mr Voss might care to come inside for once
and watch a proper closing curtain instead of shouting over it from his own tent. Mr
Voss's answer, verbatim, was unprintable. We are printing it anyway, in spirit: he will
be along presently, and he will sit in the back, and he will absolutely deny enjoying
himself.

**NOTICE — LOST PROPERTY, RECOVERED THIS EVENING FROM UNDER THE BENCHES**

One (1) wedding ring, gentleman's, engraving illegible with age and, we suspect,
sentiment. One (1) false tooth, provenance unknown, dental history not our department.
Forty-one single gloves, no pairs among them, which the management finds statistically
alarming. One child's shoe, small, well-loved, matched to no child presently on the
premises or, so far as anyone can determine, born in the last three centuries. One (1)
tortoise, deceased, believed to have arrived under its own power and to have been, in
its day, a serious professional rival of the Troupe's dog. All items may be claimed at
the ticket booth. None will be, and Management has made its peace with that.

*  *  *

**A WORD FROM MANAGEMENT**

*Best show we ever ran. Go carefully.*
— W.

*  *  *

**THE ITINERARY**

Where do we go from here? East, first — through the Bower, where they tell us the
orchards want looking at by somebody who isn't crying over them. Then wherever the road
after that turns out to run: the Divide, if the bridge will have us; the Longroad, if the
Longroad still wants marching music by the time we arrive. We have made no further plans
than that, on principle. A show that knows its whole route in advance has stopped being
a road show and started being a schedule, and we have had quite enough of schedules for
one three hundred years.

Wherever the road goes, and then the next place. That is the whole of the itinerary, and
we are printing it exactly that long on purpose.

*[in a smaller, rounder hand, wedged into the margin, plainly added after the type was
set:]*

*Flick — do the new towns have good seats? Wren wants to know before we go. I told her
you'd say every seat's a good seat but she wants the* real *answer. — W.T.*

*[and beneath it, in Flick's own hand, hurried:]*

*Best seats in the house, every one, everywhere we ever go. That's not the patter, poppet.
That's a promise.*

*  *  *

One further line, set by the printer in the smallest type on the bill, easy to miss and
not meant to be missed twice:

*Front row, centre. Retired from tonight, and every night after. Held open, in honour of
a patron who was here for every single one of the others.*

No name is given. None was thought necessary. The green outside stands empty already,
in the shape of a tent that isn't there any more, and there will be a man on it tomorrow
with a programme rolled up and put away in an inside pocket, watching the last wagon go,
who will not need to be told which seat they meant.
